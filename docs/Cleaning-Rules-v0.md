## HRV Cleaning Rules v0.1
- **Motion artifacts: drop movement bursts, keep everything else.** (Source: `analysis/001_movement-artifacts.ipynb`)
    - The participant is still for most of the session, with movement coming in short bursts, so bursts are dropped rather than grading every timestamp as low/medium/high movement.
    - A burst is any stretch where the 5-second rolling mean of rotational velocity magnitude (√(GX² + GY² + GZ²)) is above **30 deg/s**. Samples within **5 seconds** either side of a burst are also dropped, because the raw spikes extend past the smoothed stretch and the heart signal needs a few beats to settle.
    - Rotational velocity is used instead of acceleration because the EmotiBit accelerometer's resting magnitude changes with device orientation (per-axis calibration error), so "distance from 1g" would mark calm, steadily held postures as movement.
    - Result for 2.1_G1: 4 bursts dropped (85.6 s, 1.68% of the recording), all at the transitions into and out of the physical tasks (in `sart_2`, `sart_3`, `sart_5`, `sart_6`).
    - Thresholds are provisional. The result is not very sensitive to the gyro threshold (10 deg/s adds only 2 small bursts, 2.5% dropped) but is more sensitive to the padding (0.9% dropped with no padding, 2.5% with 10 s).
    - Output: `processed-data/2.1_G1_movement_mask.csv` with columns `LocalTimestamp`, `EmotiBitTimestamp`, `keep`. `LocalTimestamp` uses the same clock as `timestamp_unix` in the biometrics file; attach `keep` to HR/BI rows with a nearest-timestamp match.
- **Respiration context: drop HRV where breathing rate is outside 9–24 breaths/min.** (Source: `analysis/002_respiratory-data.ipynb`)
    - Uses the respiration belt's own `RR` reading (breaths/min) from `unofficial-data-files/2.1_respiratory_data_0.csv`. Per the belt specs, `RR` is calculated over a 30 s window and advanced every 10 s. A row with an `RR` value is a measurement, and an empty `RR` means no calculation was made at that row (not missing data). `force` is measured continuously.
    - Each RR reading is classified as **Acceptable** (12–21), **Shaky** (9–12 or 21–24) or **Unacceptable** (below 9 or above 24). The 9–24 range comes from https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.00213, with a 3 breaths/min margin inside each edge marked Shaky.
    - RR = 0 is treated as a real reading (no breaths counted in the window) and is Unacceptable, not missing.
    - Each reading is assumed to describe the 30 s *before* it, because the device calculates RR live and can only use samples it has already received. Each moment takes the quality of the first RR reading at or after it, since that reading's window always covers the moment.
    - `keep` is True for Acceptable and Shaky. It is False for Unacceptable, and for `No reading` (the last few seconds after the final RR reading, which no window covers). Shaky is kept but flagged in `resp_quality` for a closer look.
    - Result for 2.1_G1: 73.4% of recording time kept (55.3% Acceptable, 18.1% Shaky, 26.4% Unacceptable, 0.2% No reading). Unacceptable readings are mostly too slow (53 at RR = 0, 69 between 0 and 9, 13 above 24), and they coincide with large belt disturbances (every RR = 0 reading has a >10 N excursion in its window). The `task_2`/`prs_2` block is worst, with only 17% of its readings Acceptable.
    - The belt specs are not a limit for this data: 0.01 N resolution vs ~11.8 N of movement in a typical 30 s window, ~90 ms sampling vs 50 ms response time, and the 50 N ceiling is approached only once (within readings that are already Unacceptable).
    - The first 12.2 s of the biometrics recording comes before the respiratory recording starts, so it has no respiratory context.
    - The mask rule is provisional. Each moment is covered by up to 3 overlapping windows. Requiring all of them to be usable would keep 56.7% instead of 73.4%.
    - Output: `processed-data/2.1_G1_respiratory_mask.csv` with columns `timestamp_unix`, `RR`, `resp_quality`, `keep`. `timestamp_unix` uses the same clock as the biometrics file (the phase markers agree on 99.95% of samples); attach `keep` to HR/BI rows with a nearest-timestamp match.
- **PPG peak quality: detect beats from PPG Green, and drop 10-second windows whose peaks fail the quality checks.** (Source: `analysis/004_ppg-peak-detection.ipynb`)
    - The pipeline and checks come from https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12213893/. PPG Green is resampled to an even 100 Hz (the real rate is 99.57 Hz), cleaned with NeuroKit's `langevin2021` method (band-pass 0.7–3.5 Hz), and searched for peaks with its `elgendi` method. Artifact correction is off. The paper averaged all three wavelengths, and green alone is used here because it's this project's main HRV channel.
    - Each beat's interval (`PPI`, ms) is the time from the previous peak. These intervals replace the device's `BI` for HRV. They are estimates, not ground truth, because Case Study 1 has no ECG.
    - The recording is split into back-to-back 10 s windows starting at the first PPG sample. A window's intervals are all the intervals that overlap it, including the one that runs across the window's end.
    - A window is **Acceptable** if: heart rate (60 / mean interval) is 30–180 BPM, the largest interval is ≤ 3 s, largest / smallest interval is < 2.2, and the average correlation of its pulse waves with their mean wave is ≥ 0.86. Otherwise it is **Unacceptable**. There is no Shaky category, because the source's checks are pass/fail with no margin.
    - Result for 2.1_G1: 6332 beats, and 463 of 509 windows kept (91.0%, containing 90.2% of beats). The same checks (without template correlation, which needs waveform peaks) pass 31.4% of windows on the device's `BI`.
    - Cross-checks: heart rate is within 5 BPM of a spectral estimate in 83.7% of windows (device: 37.5%). Signals with no heartbeat rarely pass (white noise 0.2%, phase-scrambled green 11.2%).
    - Quality is low only in minutes 0–10 (40.0% Acceptable, at least 93.3% in every later block), so `biometric_baseline` (25.0%) and `ser_baseline` (58.8%) are the weakest labelled phases. The waveform there is irregular, so better peak detection doesn't fix it.
    - The 0.86 threshold depends on the filter: green with NeuroKit's default cleaning (0.5–8 Hz) passes only 23.0%. A different filter would need a new threshold from a source. Tightening the threshold to 0.90 gives 82.1%. The ratio threshold matters little (2.0–3.0 gives 90.4–92.3%).
    - RMSSD should be treated as low confidence even in kept windows. The paper found it insufficient against ECG (Bland-Altman ratio 0.29–0.36) in windows passing these checks, and here RMSSD from green and infrared correlates at only 0.20 across the same windows. The paper found heart rate excellent.
    - Open: why the device's beats don't line up with derived peaks, whether green or infrared gives the more accurate RMSSD, and where the sensor was worn in this study (the paper used the inner upper arm).
    - Outputs: `processed-data/2.1_G1_ppg_beats.csv`, one row per beat, with columns `LocalTimestamp`, `EmotiBitTimestamp`, `PPI`. `processed-data/2.1_G1_ppg_peak_mask.csv`, one row per window, with columns `window_start`, `window_end`, `beats_in_window`, `hr_bpm`, `max_gap_s`, `interval_ratio`, `template_corr`, `hr_ok`, `gap_ok`, `ratio_ok`, `template_ok`, `ppg_quality`, `keep`. Times use the same clock as `timestamp_unix` in the biometrics file; give each beat the window where `window_start <= LocalTimestamp < window_end`.
- **Combining the masks: a beat is Unacceptable if any mask fails it.** (Source: `analysis/999_hrv-cleaning.ipynb`)
    - Each beat in `2.1_G1_ppg_beats.csv` takes each mask's value at its own timestamp: nearest row for movement (within 0.1 s) and respiration (within 0.5 s), and the containing window for the PPG mask. A beat with no mask row nearby counts as Unacceptable for that mask.
    - **Unacceptable** if movement `keep` is False, respiration is Unacceptable or has no reading, or the PPG window is Unacceptable. **Shaky** if none of those apply but respiration is Shaky. **Acceptable** otherwise. `keep` is True for Acceptable and Shaky.
    - Result for 2.1_G1: 4150 of 6332 beats kept (65.5%; 55.9 min, 66.0% of interval time). 3197 are Acceptable and 953 Shaky. Respiration is the main limit: on its own it fails 26.4% of beats, compared with 9.8% for the PPG mask and 1.9% for movement. (With the device's `BI` and the discarded mask, 27.8% of beats were kept.)
    - `unlabeled` and `startup` have no kept data. `biometric_baseline` has 24.8 s (longest run 20.3 s) and `ser_baseline` 84.6 s (longest run 40.4 s). `prs_2` is nearly empty (60.5 s, 19.5%) because of respiration. `task_1` has the highest share among the longer phases (323.1 s, 91.3%).
    - Kept beats form 58 unbroken runs (median 30.4 s, longest 259.9 s). `segment_id` numbers them, and successive-interval HRV measures (e.g. RMSSD) should be calculated within a run.
    - Open: a minimum amount of kept data for a phase to count as usable has not been chosen yet. It should come from a source.
    - Output: `processed-data/2.1_G1_hrv_clean.csv`, one row per beat (no beats removed), with columns `LocalTimestamp`, `EmotiBitTimestamp`, `PPI`, `phase`, `movement_ok`, `RR`, `resp_quality`, `ppg_quality`, `hrv_quality`, `keep`, `segment_id`.

## Discarded Rules/Files
- **Beat-Interval beat quality: drop 10-second windows whose detected beats fail the quality checks.** (Source: `discarded/003_IBI-filtering.ipynb`)
    - The checks come from https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12213893/. They are applied to the EmotiBit's own detected beats (`BI`), because those are the intervals HRV is calculated from. Signal similarity, the paper's fourth check, is not used yet.
    - The recording is split into back-to-back 10 s windows starting at the first PPG sample. A window's intervals are all the beat intervals that overlap it, including the one that runs across the window's end, so gaps at window edges are caught.
    - A window is **Acceptable** if: heart rate (60 / mean interval) is 30–180 BPM, the largest interval is ≤ 3 s, and largest / smallest interval is < 2.2. Otherwise it is **Unacceptable**. There is no Shaky category, because the source's checks are pass/fail with no margin.
    - Result for 2.1_G1: 160 of 509 windows kept (31.4%, containing 37.5% of beats). Failures are mostly missed beats (313 of 340 ratio failures contain an interval > 1.5× the window median, and every heart-rate failure is below 30 BPM). Movement bursts account for only 10 of the 349 failed windows.
    - Detection is worst in the first ~20 min (0% Acceptable in minutes 0–10, 6.7% in 10–20). Every window in `biometric_baseline` and `ser_baseline` fails, so the baselines have no usable HRV from the device's beats.
    - Failing windows are far from the thresholds. The gap threshold makes no difference between 2.5 and 3.5 s. Loosening the ratio threshold to 3.0 would raise the kept share only to 44.4%.
    - Open: detecting peaks directly in PPG Green may recover beats the device missed. Not yet tested.
    - Output: `processed-data/2.1_G1_IBI_mask.csv`, one row per window, with columns `window_start`, `window_end`, `beats_in_window`, `hr_bpm`, `max_gap_s`, `interval_ratio`, `hr_ok`, `gap_ok`, `ratio_ok`, `ppg_quality`, `keep`. Times use the same clock as `timestamp_unix` in the biometrics file; give each HR/BI row the window where `window_start <= LocalTimestamp < window_end`.
